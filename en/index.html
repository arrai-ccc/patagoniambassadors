The model

Conservation and living, on the same side.

Parques la Patagonia is a network of private conservation parks in the Aysén Region. Within each park, a few habitable parcels of land coexist with conservation. Every purchase funds the protection of the park and its expansion into new areas — three entities, one purpose.

A symbiotic ecosystem: the sale funds the protection, the protection provides a perpetual guarantee, and the community of ambassadors sustains it and makes it grow. Every protected parcel makes the next one possible.

The guarantee

Permanent protection, independent of the owner.

The Real Right of Conservation (Chilean Law No. 20.930) sets permanent conservation conditions on the territory, no matter who owns it in the future. It is registered with the Real Estate Registrar and stays with the land forever. It is not a restriction — it is a guarantee: it ensures your surroundings remain intact.

Chilean Law No. 20.930 · Real Right of Conservation

  • Inhabit the land with construction integrated into the landscape

Frequently asked questions

What people ask us most.

Honest questions are welcome — in fact, they’re the sign of a good ambassador. Here are the most common ones; the rest we discuss directly.

If one day I want to sell my land, can I? To whom?

Yes. The land is yours, with title deed, and transfers like any property in Chile — no minimum holding period: you sell whenever you want. What travels with it is the conservation commitment, because the Real Right of Conservation is bound to the land, not to the person: whoever buys from you receives the land with the same protection rules. Your natural buyer is someone who values exactly that — surroundings that will remain intact.

Do my children inherit the land?

Yes. The land is inherited like any property in Chile: it enters your estate and passes to your heirs according to your will or the law. They receive it with the Real Right of Conservation built in — the same conditions you accepted when you entered. For many families that’s exactly the point: you don’t just leave an asset, you leave a protected territory that will still stand for the next generation.

Is there signal and internet in the territory?

Yes. The land has Starlink connectivity, which completely changed what it means to live in deep Patagonia: today you can work, communicate, and stay connected without giving up the silence and isolation of the place.

How do power and water arrive?

With solar power and spring water. The land is set up to run autonomously, with no power lines crossing the landscape and no dependence on distant grids. Living lightly on the land is part of the design, not a limitation — the base infrastructure for living is solved.

There are other projects that also sell land with conservation in Aysén. How are you different?

We know them and we’re glad they exist — the more protected land in Aysén, the better for everyone. The difference is in the architecture. In most models, the same commercial entity that sells also manages the conservation promise. We separate the roles into three: an independent, non-profit Foundation that safeguards the Real Right of Conservation, a community of ambassadors who are the owners, and the commercial arm that funds the model. The protection doesn’t depend on our goodwill — it is legally bound, with a guarantor that sells nothing.

If you care so much about conservation, why not donate the land to the State or to CONAF?

The practical: the State doesn’t have the capacity to receive, fund, and care for every valuable piece of Aysén — the national parks already operate on tight budgets, and private conservation exists to complement, not to replace.

The economic: donating the land doesn’t protect it; protecting it costs money every year, forever, and someone has to fund it. Our model makes that protection self-financing, instead of depending on subsidies or philanthropy.

And what we truly believe: conservation works better with a community that lives on and cares for the territory than with a «no trespassing» sign and no one around.

How does sponsoring a m² work? Does it go under my name?

Sponsoring a m² is a donation to the Foundation, not a land purchase — and we always say so upfront. In Chile the law does not allow titling such small areas of rural land (the legal minimum to register is half a hectare), so no one can put a square meter under your name in the Real Estate Registry. What does happen: your contribution goes to the Foundation that safeguards the park’s protection in perpetuity, and you receive a certificate with the coordinates of your m², visible on the park map, plus your place in the community of ambassadors. The meter is not yours on paper; the protection it funds is real and bound to the land forever.
